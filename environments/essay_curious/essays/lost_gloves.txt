I have a shoebox of forty-one single gloves.

It started in eighth grade, with a red mitten on the railing outside the public library. Someone had pulled it off a snowbank and set it on the rail at eye level, the way people do, so the owner could find it. I walked past it every day for three weeks. Nobody came back. On the twenty-second day I took it home, and I have been picking them up ever since.

Here is what I have learned from forty-one gloves. Children's mittens are almost always found near bus stops, because that's where small hands get distracted. Leather gloves are found outside restaurants. Work gloves, the canvas kind with rubber dots on the palm, are found in parking lots, usually flattened, and they are the saddest, because someone needed those. One glove, a black knit one with a hole in the thumb, I have found three separate times in the same block of Elm Street, and I am now fairly sure it belongs to the man who sells newspapers there, and that he keeps dropping it and someone keeps setting it on the same fence post.

I started writing down where I found each one in a notebook. Then I started drawing little maps. In tenth grade my statistics teacher let me use the data for a project, and I plotted all of them, and the clusters lined up almost exactly with the city's bus routes and the two blocks with the most restaurants. It was the first time math felt like it was about people.

I don't think I'm collecting gloves, exactly. I think I'm collecting the moment right before someone notices their hand is cold. Every glove is a small story that got interrupted. Somebody was on the phone, or chasing a kid, or getting off a bus in a hurry, and they didn't notice. I like knowing that the city is full of those moments. I like that people set lost things up on railings.

Last winter I finally gave the black knit glove back. I held it up and the newspaper man laughed and said, "That's where it went," as if it had only been gone a minute. He didn't ask how I knew it was his. I didn't explain.

I still have forty-one. The count doesn't go down; I just keep finding more.
