Every morning at 7:10, before school, I call my grandmother and read her the weather.

She doesn't need it. She lives in an assisted-living building in Ohio and goes outside maybe twice a week. But for thirty-four years she kept a weather log, a row of spiral notebooks on the shelf above her kitchen radio, one line per day: high, low, sky, and a few words. "62/41, clear, first lilacs." "88/70, haze, Tom's truck broke again." When her memory started going, the notebooks were the first thing she stopped keeping, and the last thing she stopped asking about.

So I took over. I read her the forecast for her town, then mine, and then she tells me what to write. She is very particular. "Overcast" and "cloudy" are not the same thing. If I say "nice," she asks, nice how? Some days she doesn't remember that she is the one who started the log, and asks me, politely, how long I have been doing this. I tell her a long time. That's true from both directions.

The notebooks came to me when she moved. I've read most of them. They are not a diary, but they hold a whole life if you read slowly. My mother's birth is a thunderstorm in June: "79/66, storms, Ellen came at 4." My grandfather's death is a single day with nothing written but the temperatures. Six days later she wrote "clear," and the entries went back to normal, and I have thought more about that word than about almost anything else I've ever read.

I used to think a record was something you kept so you wouldn't forget. Now I think it's something you keep so that someone else can hold the thread when you can't. My grandmother doesn't remember most of what's in those notebooks. I remember some of it for her. When I read her the high and the low, she sometimes says, out of nowhere, "Lilacs should be soon," and she's right, and neither of us knows how she knows.

This morning it was 48 and gray in her town. She said to write "overcast, cold for April." Then she asked if I'd eaten breakfast. I hadn't. I wrote it down anyway, on my own line underneath hers, which is something I've started doing. I don't know yet what my log is for. I think I'll find out by keeping it.
