The summer before my junior year, I went on a mission trip to Guatemala with my church youth group. It was an experience that completely changed my life and opened my eyes to how fortunate I am.

We spent ten days in a small village helping to build a house for a family in need. The work was very hard. It was hot every day and we had to mix the cement by hand and carry cinder blocks up a hill. At first I complained a lot because I was not used to doing physical work like that, and I missed my phone and air conditioning. But after a few days I started to get used to it and I even started to enjoy it.

The family we were building the house for had four kids. They did not have very much, but they were always so happy and grateful. The kids would play soccer with us during our breaks even though the ball was old and flat. One of the girls, Maria, always wanted to hold my hand and walk with me. Even though we did not speak the same language, we were able to communicate through smiles and laughter. It made me realize that happiness does not come from material things.

On the last day, we gave the family the keys to their new house. The mother started crying and hugged every one of us. I started crying too. It was one of the most emotional moments of my life. I knew that I had made a real difference in their lives.

When I got home, I looked at my room full of clothes and electronics and I felt guilty. I realized that I had been taking everything for granted my whole life. Now I try to be more grateful every day and I donate my old clothes and toys to people who need them.

This trip taught me that we are all the same no matter where we come from, and that we should all do our part to help those who are less fortunate. I plan to go on more mission trips in the future and to make service a big part of my life in college and beyond.
