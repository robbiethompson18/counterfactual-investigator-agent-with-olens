\section{Results across the three single-answer tasks}
\begin{frame}
  \parthead
  \begin{itemize}
    \item All of our environments that work are ideas taken from WorkspaceBench. We do not grade with pass@thousands, running a
      regex on metamodel output at many layers and tokens: we pass metamodel output to an investigator model (always Haiku 5.5,
      effort medium) and ask it to pick the correct answer, which penalizes noise and hallucinations
    \item 1,000 to 3,000 cases per category: small but statistically significant effects, probes trained on unseen data, and no
      biasing filters. WorkspaceBench filters \texttt{knowledge\_cutoff} to answers that disagree with Sonnet and Opus, which
      confounds any transcript-only baseline; we drop that filter and others like it
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Results: pooled over the three tasks}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Metamodels work on three tasks: multiple choice, knowledge cutoff, idiosyncratic preferences. \olens{} and \nla{} are
      about equally good (+3.0 and +2.6 pp over \transcript, pooled); \jlens{} does not help overall ($-$1.6 pp)
    \item All three beat their placebos (another question's reads) by statistically significant amounts; \jlens's gap is all its
      placebo misleading the investigator ($-$30.1 pp vs \transcript, against its own $-$1.6 pp)
  \end{itemize}
  \slidefig{fig/headline.pdf}
  \source{\% of cases the investigator names Qwen's answer, the three tasks weighted equally; 95\% bootstrap CIs resampling
    questions within each task, paired across conditions. Canonical menus\_inline runs, \texttt{prompt} menu.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Results: metamodels against a probe on multiple choice}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item No metamodel performs as well as a probe on multiple choice, the one of these tasks where we have one
    \item The difference is more noticeable by error rate (lower is better)
  \end{itemize}
  \slidefig{fig/headline_probes.pdf}
  \source{Multiple choice pools MMLU-Pro and SuperGPQA cases. Probe: the standard linear probe on the option newlines, nested
    two-fold by question.}
\end{frame}

\section{Multiple choice}
\begin{frame}
  \parthead
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Qwen3.6-27B (thinking off) answers multiple-choice questions from MMLU-Pro and SuperGPQA, 8 samples each; the
      investigator must name the letter Qwen picks from the prompt alone
    \item Every topic is kept; SuperGPQA's calculation questions are dropped, because Qwen writes out its working on them
      instead of giving a letter
    \item The only other filter is consistency, the same letter on at least 7 of 8 samples: 2,523 cases from a random 4,000
      MMLU-Pro questions (Qwen right on 82\%) and a random 3,000 of the 6,328 consistent questions among 10,000 sampled from
      SuperGPQA (Qwen right on 63\%)
    \item Never filtered on Qwen being wrong, so most labels are also the correct answer; the Qwen-wrong slice is reported
      separately
    \item Probe: nested two-fold cross-fitting over all consistent questions, grouped by question, each question's prediction
      from a probe trained on the other half (at most 1,000 questions); the held-out pools are not used. Valid, but training on
      a held-out pool would have been cleaner. The same protocol for essay grading
  \end{itemize}

\section{Blog authors}
\begin{frame}
  \parthead
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Blog posts (Blog Authorship Corpus, 200--450 words, one per blogger). Qwen3.6-27B (thinking off) is asked
      first ``What is my sex / How old am I most likely to be, based on the blog post I wrote below?'', then the post, then
      ``What do you think?'' and lettered options; its answer: letter log-probs averaged over two option orders
    \item Questions Qwen answers confidently and stably (both option orders agree; a typical deletion barely moves it). A case is one sentence of the post: \textbf{significant} when
      deleting it lowers P(Qwen's answer) by more than max(0.15, 5 $\times$ the post's median drop), each paired with a
      non-significant sentence of the same post (half each); \input{fig/author_n.tex}\unskip{} cases from 520 bloggers
    \item The investigator (Haiku 5.5, effort low, one turn) sees the prompt, Qwen's letter and its probability,
      and the sentence to delete, and gives P(significant)
    \item \olens: bullets with FVE at every punctuation and newline token of the question and post, layers 32/40/48,
      sampled at temperature 1. \nla: ceselder's Qwen3.6-27B NLA at the same tokens, layer 42, one greedy explanation with
      its FVE. Placebos: another post's reads (same attribute), laid onto this post's read tokens in order
